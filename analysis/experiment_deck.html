The rig & what we control

Unbalance is a vector at a plane

Two disks (0.8 kg) on a 10 mm shaft. Each disk has 8 tapped balance holes on a 30 mm radius, at 45° spacing. A calibrated screw at a hole is a known unbalance:

U = m × r  (g·mm)  ∠ θ

With r = 30 mm, 1 g = 30 g·mm. So every experiment is set by four independent knobs:

Why it matters

To trust a twin, it must be exercised across the whole envelope of that vector — every plane, magnitude, and angle — not a handful of ad-hoc runs. That demands a structured, factorial design.

Why a factorial

Crossing the axes — not sampling them randomly — means severity, phase, plane and speed effects are disentangled by construction, and any real-world unbalance is a combination the twin has already seen.

Axis 1 · why these four

Why these combinations

Any two-plane unbalance is a sum of a static (in-phase) and a couple (anti-phase) part. Covering both — plus each single plane — means the twin is trained and tested on the complete basis, so it generalizes to arbitrary real unbalance.

Why

Block B · angle

Phase characterization — 48 conditions

4 configs × 4 masses × 3 cardinal angles (90°, 180°, 270°; 0° already covered in Block A).

Masses span 0.2–1.0 g — deliberately including low masses, so angle recovery is characterized where the signal-to-noise ratio is worst, not only where it's easy.

Why

Severity tells you how much; phase tells you where. Sweeping the angle around the circle — independent of magnitude — is what lets the twin report a direction for the correction weight, not just a size.

Block C · angle resolution

Fine phase sweep — 4 conditions

Disk 1, a single mid mass (0.4 g), placed at the between-hole angles: 45°, 135°, 225°, 315°.

Fills the gaps left by the cardinal grid to check angular accuracy all the way around, at the 22.5° resolution the 8-hole pattern allows.

Why

Confirms phase accuracy isn't an artifact of always landing on a cardinal hole — the twin must be right at in-between angles too.

Axis 4 · speed

Two speeds — both sub-critical

Why two speeds

A physical unbalance is speed-independent. Estimating it at two speeds and checking they agree is a built-in internal validation — no extra ground truth needed.

Why sub-critical

At Q ≈ 12, taking a heavy mass through the 1648 rpm resonance is dangerous. Both dwells sit safely below the band; a live orbit-trip guards every run.

Rigor

Repeats & a blind hold-out

Replication

3 consecutive acquisitions at every setup capture measurement / instrument noise — essentially free, nothing is touched between them.

Blind hold-out

A colleague mounts unknown, analyst-blind conditions off-grid — the pipeline never sees the labels until after it predicts.

loaded blind
unknown faults
two-plane, unequal, off-grid angles — a true generalization test
balanced blind
empty disks
"is it balanced?" — measures the false-alarm rate (specificity)
Why

Repeats separate real effects from noise; the blind set gives an unbiased accuracy figure, and its balanced cases prove the twin doesn't invent unbalance that isn't there.
